How to Fix Bare Patches in Your Lawn

Turn ugly dirt patches into thick, green grass in just a few weekends — no fancy equipment, no landscaper, no guesswork.

Bare patches in your lawn aren't just ugly — they're an open invitation for weeds to move in. The good news: fixing them is one of the most beginner-friendly lawn projects there is, and you don't need a professional or expensive equipment to do it right.

Before you do anything, figure out WHY the patch is bare. Fixing the cause first is the difference between a patch that stays fixed and one that comes right back. The most common causes are: heavy foot traffic (a worn path across the yard), dog urine spots (small circular dead patches, often with a ring of darker green grass around the edge), too much shade (patches under trees or near fences), compacted soil (grass struggles to root in hard-packed dirt), and grubs or insect damage (patches that pull up easily like loose carpet, with no roots holding them down).

Once you know the cause, here's the basic order of operations: clear the dead grass and debris, loosen the top layer of soil, add a thin layer of compost or topsoil, spread grass seed matched to your existing lawn and your sun/shade conditions, press the seed into the soil, cover lightly with straw or seed-starter mulch, and water gently every day until it germinates.
