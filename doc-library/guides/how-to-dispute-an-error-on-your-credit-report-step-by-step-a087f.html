How to Dispute an Error on Your Credit Report (Step-by-Step)

A mistake on your credit report can quietly cost you thousands in higher interest rates — here is the exact, free process to get it fixed, using your legal rights under federal law.

Finding a wrong late payment, an account that is not yours, or an old debt that should have dropped off your report can feel like a punch to the gut — especially when you know it is hurting your score for no good reason. The good news: fixing it is completely free, and federal law is on your side. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), credit bureaus are legally required to investigate any error you report and correct or remove anything that cannot be verified as accurate. You do not need a lawyer, a credit repair company, or any special connections — just the right documents and a clear paper trail. Here is exactly how it works, from pulling your report to getting the final confirmation letter.

Step 1: Get your credit reports first. You are entitled to a free copy from all three major bureaus — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — through the one official site authorized by federal law: AnnualCreditReport.com. Avoid any other "free credit report" site; most either charge hidden fees or try to sell you something.
