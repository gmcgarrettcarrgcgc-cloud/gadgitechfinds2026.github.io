How to Reset the Tire Pressure Light on a Toyota Camry (Without a Shop Visit)

That glowing tire pressure warning light doesn't mean you need a mechanic - in most cases you can clear it yourself in under 15 minutes, right from your driver's seat.

If the Tire Pressure Monitoring System (TPMS) light just came on in your Toyota Camry, take a breath - this is one of the most common (and easiest) warning lights to deal with yourself. Before you do anything else, walk around your car and check all four tires (plus the spare, if yours has a sensor) for anything obviously wrong: a nail, a flat, or a tire that looks noticeably lower than the others. The light exists to warn you about pressure, so pressure is always step one.

Next, find your car's correct tire pressure. Do NOT use the number printed on the tire's sidewall - that's the tire's maximum rating, not what Toyota recommends. The correct number is on a sticker inside the driver's door frame (open the door and look at the edge of the door or the pillar behind it). It usually reads something like "32 PSI front / 32 PSI rear," though it can vary by trim and tire size.

Using a tire pressure gauge (a $5-10 tool from any auto parts store, or many gas station air pumps have one built in), check all four tires when they're "cold" - meaning the car hasn't been driven for at least 3 hours, or driven less than a mile.
