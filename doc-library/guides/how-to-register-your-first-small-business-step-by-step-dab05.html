Small Business Basics

How to Register Your First Small Business (Step by Step)

Turning your side hustle into an official business feels overwhelming — but it is really just five simple steps, and this guide walks you through every one of them in plain English.

So you have decided to make it official. Maybe you have been selling crafts, doing freelance work, or dreaming up the next big idea, and now you are ready to register an actual business. Good news: this process is way less scary than it sounds, and you can do most of it yourself without paying a lawyer hundreds of dollars.

Here is the truth nobody tells beginners: registering a business is not one big complicated event. It is a handful of small, specific tasks done in the right order. Skip a step or do them out of order, and you will end up confused, delayed, or paying fees twice. Do them in order, and you will have a legitimate, legal business in as little as a week.

STEP 1: CHOOSE YOUR BUSINESS STRUCTURE

Before you register anything, you need to decide what "type" of business you are forming. The three most common options for a first-time small business owner are:

- Sole Proprietorship: The simplest option. You and the business are legally the same entity. No separate registration is required in most states beyond a "Doing Business As" (DBA) filing if you use a name other than your own legal name. Downside: your personal assets (car, house, savings) are not protected if the business gets sued or goes into debt.

- LLC (Limited Liability Company): The most popular choice for small businesses. It separates you from your business legally, so your personal assets are protected in most situations. Costs more to set up (usually $50-$500 depending on your state) and may have small annual fees.

- Corporation (S-Corp or C-Corp): More complex, more paperwork, generally for businesses planning to raise investment money or with multiple owners. Most first-time small business owners do not need this yet.

Recommendation for beginners: unless you have a specific reason to pick something else, an LLC is usually the sweet spot of protection and simplicity for a first-time owner planning to take on any risk (selling products, providing services, signing contracts).
