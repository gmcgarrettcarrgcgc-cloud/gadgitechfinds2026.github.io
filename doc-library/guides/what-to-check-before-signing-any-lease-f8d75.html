Renting & Real Estate Basics

What to Check Before Signing Any Lease

Before you sign your name on that lease, spend 20 minutes with this checklist — it could save you thousands of dollars and months of headaches.

Signing a lease is exciting. You found a place, you can picture your furniture in it, and you just want to move in already. But a lease is a legally binding contract, and once you sign it, you are agreeing to everything written inside — even the stuff you skimmed past. Most renters never read the whole thing. Most renters also get surprised later by a fee, a rule, or a policy they did not know existed. This guide walks you through exactly what to check before you sign, in plain English, so you avoid the common traps.

First, always get the lease in writing before you hand over any money. A verbal promise from a landlord ("do not worry, I will fix that before you move in") means nothing if it is not in the lease itself. If a landlord promises something, ask them to add it as a written addendum and sign that too.

Second, check the actual dollar amounts line by line. Rent amount, due date, late fee amount, and grace period (how many days late you can be before a fee kicks in) should all be spelled out in exact numbers — not vague language like "reasonable late fee."
