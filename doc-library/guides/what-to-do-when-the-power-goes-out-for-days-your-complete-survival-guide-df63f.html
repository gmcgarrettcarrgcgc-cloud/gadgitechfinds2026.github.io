What to Do When the Power Goes Out for Days: Your Complete Survival Guide

A storm just took out the power and the utility company says it could be days, not hours. Here is the exact, safe, step-by-step plan to keep your family fed, warm, and calm until the lights come back on.

The first hour after a long-term power outage is the most important one — what you do (or do not do) in those first 60 minutes decides whether your food survives, your pipes stay safe, and your family stays comfortable for the days ahead.

Step 1: Confirm it is not just you. Check a neighbor house or your phone carrier map. If it is a wide outage, settle in for the long haul instead of waiting it out minute by minute.

Step 2: Protect your food immediately. Keep the refrigerator and freezer doors closed. A full freezer holds a safe temperature for about 48 hours; a half-full one for about 24 hours. Every time you open the door, you lose precious cold air, so resist the urge to check.

Step 3: Gather your light and communication tools before it gets dark. Flashlights and battery or hand-crank radios are safer than candles, which are a leading cause of house fires during outages.

Step 4: Unplug or turn off major appliances like your stove, AC unit, and computer. This protects them from a power surge when electricity comes back on, and prevents your circuits from being overloaded all at once.
