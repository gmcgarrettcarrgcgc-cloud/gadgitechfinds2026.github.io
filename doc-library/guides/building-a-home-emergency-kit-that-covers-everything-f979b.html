Emergency Weather Prep

Building a Home Emergency Kit That Covers Everything

Power outages, storms, and sudden emergencies do not wait for a convenient time — this guide walks you through building a complete home emergency kit, step by step, so your family is ready for anything.

When the power goes out or a storm warning hits your phone, the worst time to start thinking about supplies is right then. A well-stocked emergency kit means you can stay calm, stay safe, and stay comfortable no matter what happens outside. The good news: you do not need to be an expert or spend a fortune to build one. You just need a system.

Step 1: Pick Your Container
Start with a large plastic storage bin with a lid, a duffel bag, or a backpack for each family member. Keep it somewhere easy to grab — a hall closet, mudroom, or garage shelf works well. Avoid basements that could flood.

Step 2: Water — The Non-Negotiable First Item
Store at least 1 gallon of water per person, per day, for a minimum of 3 days (that is 3 gallons per person). If you have space, aim for a 2-week supply. Use sealed, store-bought water when possible, and rotate it every 6-12 months.

Step 3: Food That Does Not Need Cooking
Choose non-perishable items you can eat without power or water: canned meats, fruits, and vegetables, peanut butter, crackers, granola bars, and dried fruit. Pack a manual can opener — this is the item people forget most often.
