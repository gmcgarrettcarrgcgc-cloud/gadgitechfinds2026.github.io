Hunting Basics

Basic Hunting Safety Every Beginner Must Know

One careless moment with a firearm or one missed step off a tree stand is how good hunting trips turn into tragedies — here are the non-negotiable safety habits every beginner needs before they ever step into the woods.

Hunting is one of the most rewarding outdoor traditions there is, but it comes with real risks if you skip the basics. The good news: almost every hunting accident is preventable, and it comes down to a small handful of habits that experienced hunters practice automatically. This guide walks you through exactly what those habits are, starting with the single most important thing you should do before you ever pick up a firearm: get properly certified.

1. Get Certified Before You Go
Nearly every U.S. state requires a hunter education course before you can buy a hunting license, especially if you were born after a certain year. Even if you are exempt, take the course anyway. These classes — often free or very low-cost, and increasingly available online plus a short in-person field day — cover firearm handling, wildlife identification, and state-specific laws in a few hours. It is the fastest way to learn the fundamentals correctly instead of picking up bad habits from guesswork.
