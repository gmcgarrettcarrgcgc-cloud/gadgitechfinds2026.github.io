How to Roll Over a 401k When You Change Jobs

Changing jobs can be a stressful and overwhelming experience, and one of the most important financial decisions you'll need to make during this time is what to do with your 401k plan. If you're like many Americans, you've worked hard to build up your retirement savings, and you'll want to make sure you don't lose any of that money when you switch jobs. With this guide, you'll learn how to roll over your 401k and take control of your financial future.

To roll over a 401k when you change jobs, you'll need a few basic materials and some knowledge of the process. Here's a step-by-step guide to help you get started:
1. Gather your documents: You'll need to have your 401k account information, including your account balance and any outstanding loan balances. You'll also need to have your new employer's 401k plan information, if you plan to roll your old 401k into your new employer's plan.
2. Choose the right type of account: You have a few options when it comes to rolling over your 401k, including rolling it into your new employer's 401k plan, rolling it into an IRA, or cashing it out. Rolling it into your new employer's 401k plan can be a good option if you like the investment options and fees associated with the new plan. Rolling it into an IRA can give you more control over your investments and potentially lower fees. Cashing it out should be a last resort, as you'll have to pay taxes and potentially penalties on the withdrawal.
3. Contact your old 401k plan administrator: You'll need to contact your old 401k plan administrator to let them know that you want to roll over your account. They'll provide you with the necessary paperwork and instructions on how to complete the rollover.
4. Fill out the paperwork: You'll need to fill out the paperwork provided by your old 401k plan administrator, which will include information about your account balance, your new account information, and your rollover options.
5. Choose your investments: Once you've rolled over your 401k, you'll need to choose your investments. If you're rolling it into an IRA, you can choose from a wide range of investments, including stocks, bonds, and mutual funds. If you're rolling it into your new employer's 401k plan, you'll be limited to the investment options available in that plan.
6. Monitor your account: Once you've rolled over your 401k, you'll need to monitor your account to make sure everything is in order. You should receive a confirmation statement from your new account administrator, which will show your account balance and any fees associated with the account.
Common mistakes to avoid when rolling over a 401k include:
- Cashing out your 401k instead of rolling it over, which can result in taxes and penalties
- Not considering all of your options, including rolling it into an IRA or your new employer's 401k plan
- Not monitoring your account after the rollover, which can result in lost or misplaced funds
Tips for best results:
- Take your time and don't rush the process
- Consider consulting with a financial advisor to get personalized advice
- Make sure you understand all of the fees associated with your new account
- Consider consolidating your accounts to simplify your financial life
What you need to get started:
- Your 401k account information
- Your new employer's 401k plan information (if applicable)
- A check or other payment method to cover any fees associated with the rollover
- A computer or phone with internet access to research and compare different investment options
By following these steps and avoiding common mistakes, you can roll over your 401k and take control of your retirement savings. Remember to take your time and don't rush the process, and consider consulting with a financial advisor to get personalized advice.
