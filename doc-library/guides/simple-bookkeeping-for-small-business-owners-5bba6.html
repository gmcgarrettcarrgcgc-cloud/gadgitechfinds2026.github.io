Simple Bookkeeping for Small Business Owners

Stop dreading your books. This guide gives you a dead-simple system to track every dollar in and out, so tax time is easy and you always know if you are actually making money.

If you started a small business because you love the work, not the paperwork, you are not alone. Most business owners feel the same way about bookkeeping. But here is the good news: you do not need an accounting degree or expensive software to keep clean books. You just need a simple, consistent system.

Bookkeeping is really just answering two questions, over and over: "How much money came in?" and "How much money went out?" If you can answer those two questions clearly at any moment, you are doing bookkeeping right.

1. Separate your business and personal money first
Before you track a single transaction, open a separate bank account (and if you have one, a separate business debit or credit card) just for your business. This is the single most important step. Mixing personal and business money is the number one reason bookkeeping becomes a nightmare later. Even if you are a one-person freelance operation, a free business checking account at almost any bank solves this instantly.

2. Pick one simple system and stick with it
You have three realistic options: a notebook/paper ledger, a spreadsheet (free with Google Sheets), or beginner-friendly software (like Wave, which is free, or QuickBooks Simple Start). For most beginners, a spreadsheet is the sweet spot — powerful enough to be useful, simple enough not to overwhelm you.
