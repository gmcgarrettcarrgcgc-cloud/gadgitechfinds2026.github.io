Killing Weeds Without Killing Your Grass

Tired of weed killer taking out your dandelions AND your lawn? Here's the simple, safe system for wiping out weeds while keeping your grass thick, green, and untouched.

If you've ever sprayed a "weed killer" and watched your grass turn yellow right along with the weeds, you're not alone — it's the single most common lawn care mistake. The good news: killing weeds without hurting your grass isn't complicated once you understand one key idea — selective vs. non-selective herbicides.

A non-selective herbicide (like most glyphosate-based products) kills ANY plant it touches — grass included. A selective herbicide is formulated to kill certain plants (like broadleaf weeds — dandelions, clover, plantain) while leaving grass alone. Almost everything in this guide centers on choosing the right selective product for your exact weed and grass type, and applying it the right way.

Step 1: Identify your grass type first. Is it a cool-season grass (Kentucky bluegrass, fescue, ryegrass — common in the North) or a warm-season grass (Bermuda, zoysia, St. Augustine — common in the South)? This matters because some herbicides that are safe on one type will damage the other. Check your grass type before buying anything — most bag/bottle labels list which grass types a product is safe for, right on the front or back.
