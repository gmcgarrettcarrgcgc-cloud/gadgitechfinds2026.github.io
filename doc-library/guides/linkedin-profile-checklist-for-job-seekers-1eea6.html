LinkedIn Profile Checklist for Job Seekers

Your LinkedIn profile is often the first impression recruiters have of you. This checklist ensures every section works together to get you noticed and land interviews.

## LinkedIn Profile Checklist for Job Seekers

### 1. Profile Photo

Your photo is the first thing someone sees. It takes 7 seconds to form an impression.

**The rules:**
— Use a photo of just your head and shoulders (headshot, not a selfie, not a full-body photo, not a photo with other people)
— Wear what you'd wear to an interview in your industry
— Smile naturally. You're approachable, not stiff
— Use good lighting. If your bedroom lighting is dim, take it outdoors or near a window
— Use a plain, simple background. Busy backgrounds distract from your face
— Make sure your face takes up 60-70% of the frame
— Avoid sunglasses, hats, filters, or heavy makeup

**Don't use:**
— A photo from your vacation
— A photo from a party
— A photo with someone else cropped out
— A professional headshot from 10 years ago (update every 2-3 years)

If you don't have a good photo, ask a friend with a decent phone camera to take one. A $0 photo with good lighting beats a $200 photo in bad lighting.

### 2. Headline

Your headline appears next to your name and is what shows up in recruiter searches. Most people just write their job title. That's a mistake.

**What to write:**
Instead of "Marketing Manager," write something like "Marketing Manager | B2B SaaS | Content Strategy | Data-Driven Growth."

Include:
— Your current job title
— Your industry or specialization
— 2-3 key skills or areas you want to be known for

**Why it matters:**
Recruiters search LinkedIn by keywords. If you only write your job title, you show up for fewer searches. If you include relevant keywords (content strategy, data analytics, healthcare recruiting, whatever your field is), more recruiters find you.

**Examples:**
— "Graphic Designer | Branding | UX/UI | Figma"
— "Sales Development Rep | SaaS | Outbound Growth | Pipeline Generation"
— "Nurse Manager | Pediatrics | Staff Leadership | Quality Outcomes"
— "Financial Analyst | Budgeting | Forecasting | Healthcare Finance"

Use all 220 characters if you can. You're not being boastful — you're being findable.

### 3. About Section

This is where you tell your story. Most people leave it blank or write something generic. That's a missed chance.

**What to write:**
2-3 short paragraphs (50-100 words total). Think of it as your elevator pitch.

**Include:**
— What you do now (your current role or what you're looking for)
— What you're good at (specific skills, not just "hard-working")
— Why it matters (what problems do you solve?)
— A call to action (what should people do next?)

**Example:**
"I'm a customer success manager at a growing healthcare SaaS company. I specialize in onboarding new clients and reducing churn through proactive support. I love working with teams to fix processes and improve customer outcomes. If you're hiring for CS roles or want to discuss customer retention strategies, let's connect."

**Don't write:**
— "Looking for opportunities" (vague)
— Your entire life story (too long)
— Buzzwords without examples ("results-driven," "team player," "hard-working")

### 4. Experience Section

Recruiters spend the most time here. Make every line count.

**For each job, include:**
— Job title
— Company name
— Employment dates (month and year, not just year)
— A 2-3 sentence description of what you did
— 3-5 bullet points showing impact (not just tasks)

**What matters:**
Don't list tasks. List results.

**Bad:** "Responsible for managing social media accounts and creating posts."

**Good:** "Grew Instagram following from 10K to 65K followers in 8 months through data-driven content strategy. Increased engagement rate by 140% and drove 2,300 website clicks from social in Q3 2024."

**Include numbers whenever possible:**
— % increase in sales, efficiency, or engagement
— How many people or projects you managed
— Revenue generated or costs saved
