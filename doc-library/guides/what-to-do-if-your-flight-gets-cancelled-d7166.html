What to Do If Your Flight Gets Cancelled

Your flight just got cancelled. Here's the exact playbook to rebook, get compensated, and get where you're going — without stress or leaving money on the table.

## What to Do If Your Flight Gets Cancelled

### 1. Stay Calm and Gather Information (First 5 Minutes)

The moment you get the cancellation alert (text, app, or gate announcement), do not panic. Your job right now is information.

**Step 1: Screenshot or photograph the cancellation notice**
Your phone or email will show the flight number, date, original time, and reason for cancellation (weather, mechanical, crew scheduling, etc.). Take a screenshot. This is your proof of cancellation time and the airline's official reason. Save it immediately.

**Step 2: Find your booking confirmation**
You'll need the confirmation number from when you booked. It's usually in your email or airline app. Take a screenshot of that too. Write down: flight number, original departure time, and your name exactly as it appears on your ID.

**Step 3: Check your ticket type**
Open your airline app or email and look for the fare rules. Was this a basic economy, main cabin, or first-class ticket? Was it refundable or non-refundable? This determines what the airline will offer you. Refundable tickets give you more leverage.

**Step 4: Note the reason for cancellation**
Did the airline say "weather," "mechanical," "crew scheduling," or "operational"? Write it down. Weather cancellations have different rules than airline-caused cancellations.

### 2. Contact the Airline Immediately (First 30 Minutes)

Do not wait in the airport line. Digital contact is faster.

**Step 1: Use the airline app or website**
Open the airline app on your phone. Look for "My Trips" or "Manage Booking." Tap your flight. You'll see a "Flight Cancelled" notice with options to rebook or get a refund. Click this immediately. The app will show available flights automatically.

**Step 2: Call if the app doesn't work**
If the app is overloaded or frozen (common during mass cancellations), call the airline's customer service line. You can find it on your confirmation email, the airline's website, or by Googling "[airline name] customer service." Have your confirmation number ready.

**Step 3: Be direct and polite**
Tell them: "My flight [number] on [date] was cancelled. I need to be rebooked on the next available flight to [destination], regardless of airline."

Most agents will try to rebook you on the airline's own flights three to five days later. Push back. Say: "I need the next available flight today, even if it's with a competitor. I'm entitled to that." They have this authority.

**Step 4: Get a confirmation number for your new flight**
Once rebooked, the agent will give you a new confirmation number and flight details. Write it down. Ask them to email it to you. Repeat your destination and new flight time back to them to confirm.

### 3. Understand Your Legal Rights (While on Hold)

In the US, the rules come from the Department of Transportation (DOT). In Europe, it's EU Regulation 261/2004.

**US Rules (DOT)**
If a US airline cancels your flight and it's not weather-related, they must rebook you on the next available flight (their airline or a competitor) at no extra charge. You're also entitled to a refund of your ticket cost if you don't want to be rebooked.

**European Rules (EU 261/2004)**
If an airline cancels a flight departing from the EU, you're entitled to compensation: €250 for flights under 1,500 km, €400 for 1,500-3,500 km, and €600 for flights over 3,500 km.
