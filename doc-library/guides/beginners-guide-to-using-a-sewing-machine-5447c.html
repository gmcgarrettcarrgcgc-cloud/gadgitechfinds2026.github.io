Sewing & Mending

Beginner's Guide to Using a Sewing Machine

Stop being intimidated by that sewing machine in your closet — in one sitting, you will go from "what does this dial even do?" to sewing your first straight, confident seam.

So you have got a sewing machine — maybe it was a gift, a garage sale find, or it has been sitting in a closet for years — and you have no idea where to start. That is completely normal. Every experienced sewist started exactly where you are right now, staring at a machine full of knobs, dials, and a needle that looks scarier than it is.

Here is the good news: modern sewing machines are built to be simple once you understand the basic parts and the basic motion. You do not need to know how to sew clothes from scratch, follow complicated patterns, or own an expensive machine. You just need to understand five things: how to thread the machine, how to wind and load a bobbin, how to control your stitching, how to fix the two most common beginner mistakes, and how to practice safely.

1. Know Your Machine Basic Parts

Before touching any buttons, find these five parts on your machine:

- Spool pin: a small pin, usually on top, that holds your spool of thread.
- Handwheel: the round dial on the right side of the machine — turning it by hand raises and lowers the needle.
- Presser foot: the metal piece that holds fabric flat against the machine while you sew. It has a lever behind it to lift it up and down.
- Bobbin: a small spool that holds the bottom thread, hidden under the needle plate.
- Foot pedal: the pedal on the floor that controls how fast the machine sews — press gently for slow, press harder for fast.

Take two minutes just to locate these five things. You will refer back to them constantly.
