Sewing On a Button That Never Falls Off Again

Stop losing buttons at the worst possible moment. Learn the simple, unbreakable stitch method that keeps buttons attached for years, not weeks.

Losing a button always seems to happen at the worst time — right before a meeting, a date, or a job interview. The good news is that sewing on a button that actually stays put is one of the easiest skills you can learn, and you do not need any special training or expensive tools.

Most buttons fall off not because the thread breaks, but because they were sewn on wrong in the first place. A button sewn flat against the fabric, with no room to move, puts stress on the threads every time you push the button through the buttonhole. Over time, that stress snaps the thread. The fix is something called a "thread shank" — a small stack of thread between the button and the fabric that gives the button room to flex. This one trick is the difference between a button that falls off in a week and one that lasts as long as the shirt.

Before you start, gather these supplies: a needle, thread that matches (or is close to) the fabric color, small scissors, the button itself, and something to use as a spacer — a toothpick, a matchstick, or even a second needle laid flat on top of the button works perfectly.

1. Thread your needle and prepare it.
Cut a piece of thread about 18-24 inches long. Thread it through the eye of the needle, then pull the two ends together so you have a doubled thread. Tie a small knot at the end. Doubling the thread makes it much stronger, which is one of the biggest secrets to a button that lasts.
