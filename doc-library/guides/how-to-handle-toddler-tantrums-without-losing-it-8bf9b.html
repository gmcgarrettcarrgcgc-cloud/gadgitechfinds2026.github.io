How to Handle Toddler Tantrums Without Losing It

Toddler tantrums are normal, but they don't have to derail your day — learn the science-backed strategies that actually work and keep your cool in the chaos.

Tantrums are a sign your toddler's big feelings are bigger than their ability to express them. They're not trying to manipulate you or test your patience — their brain is literally overwhelmed. The good news: tantrums are a developmental phase that peaks around age 2-3 and gets better with the right approach.

The worst thing you can do during a tantrum is match your toddler's intensity. When you stay calm, you're teaching them that big feelings don't require panic. Your job isn't to stop the tantrum — it's to keep everyone safe, stay present, and let it pass. Most tantrums last 5-10 minutes if you don't fuel them with anger or punishment.

This guide shows you exactly how to predict tantrums before they happen, what to do when one hits, and how to build your toddler's emotional skills so fewer tantrums happen over time.
