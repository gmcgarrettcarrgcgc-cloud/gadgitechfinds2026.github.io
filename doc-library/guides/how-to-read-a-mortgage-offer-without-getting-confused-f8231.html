How to Read a Mortgage Offer Without Getting Confused

Mortgage paperwork looks like it was designed to intimidate you — but once you know what 8 numbers actually mean, you can compare offers like a pro and spot a bad deal in seconds.

Getting a mortgage offer (sometimes called a Loan Estimate) in the mail or your inbox can feel like opening a legal exam you never studied for. Pages of percentages, fees, and acronyms — and somewhere buried in there is whether this is actually a good deal for you.

Here is the good news: every mortgage offer in the U.S. is required by law to use the same standard format, called a Loan Estimate. That means once you learn where to look, you can read ANY lender offer the same way, every time.

Step 1: Find the interest rate — but do not stop there.
The interest rate is on page 1, usually top-left. This is the rate you pay on the money you borrow. But the interest rate alone does not tell you the true cost of the loan — that is what Step 2 is for.

Step 2: Find the APR (Annual Percentage Rate).
The APR is usually on page 3. It includes the interest rate PLUS lender fees, spread out over the life of the loan. This is the single best number for comparing two different offers, because it reflects the TOTAL cost, not just the interest rate. If two lenders quote the same interest rate but one has a much higher APR, that lender is charging more in fees.

Step 3: Check the loan term and type.
Look for "30-year fixed," "15-year fixed," or "5-year ARM" (adjustable-rate mortgage). A fixed rate never changes. An ARM can change after an initial period — meaning your payment could go up later.
