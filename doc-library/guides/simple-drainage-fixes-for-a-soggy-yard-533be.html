Simple Drainage Fixes for a Soggy Yard

Tired of a swampy backyard after every rainstorm? These easy, budget-friendly fixes will get water moving away from your yard for good — no excavator required.

If your lawn turns into a mud pit every time it rains, or you've got that one spot that never seems to dry out, you're dealing with a drainage problem. The good news: most yard drainage issues are fixable by a total beginner with basic tools and a weekend of work.

First, figure out where the water is actually coming from and where it's pooling. Walk your yard right after a rain (or while it's raining, in boots) and watch where water collects and which direction it flows. Look for four common culprits: compacted soil that won't absorb water, low spots that trap water like a bowl, gutters dumping water right next to your foundation, and neighboring yards or slopes draining onto your property.

Once you know your problem area, you can match it to the right fix. A single low spot might just need regrading. A whole soggy lawn might need aeration. A wet patch near your house almost always means your gutters need attention first — that's the cheapest fix and the one people skip most often.
