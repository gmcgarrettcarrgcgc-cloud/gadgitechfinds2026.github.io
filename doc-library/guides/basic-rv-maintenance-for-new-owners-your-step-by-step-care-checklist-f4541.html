Basic RV Maintenance for New Owners: Your Step-by-Step Care Checklist

Skip the $500 repair bill — learn the simple checks every RV owner should do before, during, and after every trip.

So you just got your first RV — congratulations! Before you hit the road, there are a handful of simple checks that can save you from breakdowns, leaks, and expensive repairs down the line. The good news: none of this requires special tools or mechanical experience. You just need to know what to look for and how often to look.

1. Check Your Tires Every Time
Tires are the single most common cause of roadside RV emergencies. Before every trip:
- Check tire pressure with a tire gauge when tires are cold (haven't been driven on yet that day). Your RV's exact recommended pressure is printed on a sticker inside the driver's door or in the owner's manual — always use that number, not a guess.
- Look for cracks, bulges, or uneven wear on the sidewalls.
- RV tires age even if they look fine — check the manufacture date (a 4-digit code stamped on the sidewall, like "2321" for the 23rd week of 2021). Most experts recommend replacing RV tires after 5-7 years regardless of tread depth, since rubber degrades with age and sun exposure.
