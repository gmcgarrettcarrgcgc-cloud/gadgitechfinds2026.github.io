Canning and Preserving Your Harvest: The Beginner's Safety-First Guide

Turn your garden bounty into shelf-stable jars that last all winter - without the guesswork or risk. Learn the exact safe methods that keep your food delicious AND bacteria-free.

Canning sounds intimidating, but it's just controlled cooking in sealed jars. The science is simple: heat kills bacteria, the seal keeps new bacteria out, and your food stays fresh for a year.

There are two safe methods for beginners. Water-bath canning works for high-acid foods (jams, pickles, tomato sauce). Pressure canning is for low-acid foods (beans, corn, meats). This guide focuses on water-bath canning because it requires no special equipment beyond what you probably have.

The three golden rules:

1. Always use tested, published recipes - never guess at ingredient ratios or cooking times. Bad ratios let dangerous bacteria survive.

2. Use the exact equipment specified in your recipe (mason jars, lids, canning pots).

3. Follow times and temperatures exactly. They're science, not suggestions.

If you follow these rules, canning is safe, rewarding, and will give you months of preserved food.
