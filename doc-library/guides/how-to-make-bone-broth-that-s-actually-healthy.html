How to Make Bone Broth That's Actually Healthy

If you're looking for a nutritious and delicious way to boost your health, making bone broth from scratch is an excellent place to start. With its rich, velvety texture and deep, satisfying flavor, bone broth is a staple in many health-conscious kitchens. But with so many recipes and methods out there, it can be hard to know where to start - that's why you need this guide to making healthy bone broth.

To make healthy bone broth, you'll need a few basic materials and some patience. Here's what you'll need to get started:
- 2-4 pounds of beef, chicken, or fish bones (you can use a combination of bones for added flavor and nutrition)
- 2-4 carrots, chopped
- 2-4 celery stalks, chopped
- 2-4 cloves of garlic, minced
- 1 large onion, chopped
- 6-8 quarts of water
- 1 tablespoon of apple cider vinegar (optional)
- A large stockpot (at least 8 quarts in size)
- Cheesecloth or a fine-mesh strainer
- A blender or food processor (optional)

Now, let's move on to the step-by-step process for making healthy bone broth:
1. **Prepare your bones**: If you're using raw bones, you'll need to roast them in the oven before making your broth. Preheat your oven to 400F (200C), and place the bones on a baking sheet. Roast the bones for 30 minutes to 1 hour, or until they're lightly browned. This step helps to bring out the natural flavors of the bones and creates a richer, more complex broth.
2. **Chop your vegetables**: While your bones are roasting, chop your carrots, celery, and onion. You can also mince your garlic during this time. These vegetables will add flavor and nutrients to your broth, so be sure to use fresh, organic produce whenever possible.
3. **Combine your ingredients**: Once your bones are done roasting, remove them from the oven and place them in your large stockpot. Add the chopped vegetables, garlic, and water to the pot, making sure that all of the ingredients are fully submerged.
4. **Add acidity**: If you're using apple cider vinegar, now is the time to add it to the pot. The acidity of the vinegar helps to draw out the minerals from the bones, creating a more nutritious broth.
5. **Bring to a boil**: Place the pot over high heat and bring the mixture to a boil. Once boiling, reduce the heat to a simmer and let the broth cook for 12-24 hours. The longer you cook the broth, the more collagen and minerals will be extracted from the bones.
6. **Strain the broth**: After the cooking time has elapsed, remove the pot from the heat and let it cool slightly. Strain the broth through cheesecloth or a fine-mesh strainer into a large bowl or container. Discard the solids and reserve the broth.
7. **Chill and skim**: Let the broth cool to room temperature, then cover it and refrigerate it. Once chilled, skim off any solidified fat that has risen to the top of the broth. This will help to create a clearer, more flavorful broth.
8. **Blend or puree (optional)**: If you want a creamy, smooth broth, you can blend or puree it in a blender or food processor. This step is optional, but it can help to create a more velvety texture.
9. **Store your broth**: Transfer the broth to airtight containers and store it in the fridge for up to 5 days or in the freezer for up to 6 months. You can also can the broth using a pressure canner for longer-term storage.

Common mistakes to avoid when making bone broth include:
- Not roasting the bones before making the broth, which can result in a less flavorful and less nutritious final product.
- Not using enough water, which can lead to a concentrated, bitter broth.
- Not cooking the broth for a long enough period, which can result in a broth that's low in collagen and minerals.
- Not straining the broth properly, which can leave behind solids and create a cloudy, unappetizing final product.

Tips for best results include:
- Using a variety of bones for added flavor and nutrition.
- Adding other ingredients to the broth, such as herbs and spices, for added flavor.
- Experimenting with different cooking times and temperatures to find your perfect broth.
- Using a slow cooker or Instant Pot to make the broth, which can simplify the process and reduce cooking time.
