How to Field Dress a Deer Step by Step

Just tagged your first deer and have no idea what happens next? This step-by-step guide walks you through field dressing safely, cleanly, and correctly — no guesswork, no ruined meat.

Field dressing means removing the internal organs from a deer as soon as possible after the kill, so the meat cools properly and stays clean and safe to eat. Here is what you need before you start: a sharp fixed-blade knife (3-4 inches is plenty), a pair of nitrile or rubber gloves, your tag, and a clear flat spot to work.

Step 1: Tag the deer first. In most states, you must tag your deer immediately or before you move it — check your state specific rule before you do anything else, since this varies and getting it wrong can mean a real fine.

Step 2: Put on your gloves. This is not optional. Deer can carry bacteria and diseases (including Chronic Wasting Disease), and gloves protect you during every step that follows.

Step 3: Position the deer on its back if possible, with the head slightly uphill. Spread the back legs so you have clear access to the belly.
