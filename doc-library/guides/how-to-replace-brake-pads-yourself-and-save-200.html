How to Replace Brake Pads Yourself and Save $200

If you're tired of throwing away money on car repairs, learning to replace your own brake pads can be a game-changer. Not only will you save around $200, but you'll also gain a valuable skill that will make you more self-sufficient and confident as a car owner. With the right guidance, you can replace your brake pads in just a few hours, even if you have no prior experience with car repairs.

Replacing your own brake pads is a relatively simple process that can save you a significant amount of money. Here's what you need to get started:
- A set of new brake pads (available at most auto parts stores or online)
- A C-clamp or lug wrench (for compressing the caliper piston)
- A socket set or wrench set (for removing the caliper and rotor screws)
- A pair of gloves and safety glasses (for protecting yourself from brake dust and other debris)
- A car jack and some jack stands (for lifting the vehicle and supporting it while you work)
- A wheel chock (for securing the opposite wheel and preventing the vehicle from rolling)
Before you begin, make sure you have a basic understanding of how your vehicle's braking system works, and that you're comfortable with using tools and working with your hands.

Here are the steps to follow:
1. Prepare your vehicle by finding a level and stable surface to park on, and applying the parking brake. Put the vehicle in park (if it's an automatic) or first gear (if it's a manual), and engage the wheel chock to secure the opposite wheel.
2. Jack up the vehicle and support it with jack stands, making sure it's stable and secure. Remove the wheel to access the brake caliper and rotor.
3. Remove the caliper screws and slide the caliper off the rotor. Be careful not to damage the brake hose or any other components.
4. Remove the old brake pads from the caliper, taking note of how they're positioned and secured. You may need to use a little force to pull them out, but be careful not to damage the caliper or rotor.
5. Inspect the caliper and rotor for any signs of wear or damage, and clean them out with a wire brush or some brake cleaner. This will help ensure that the new pads seat properly and function smoothly.
6. Install the new brake pads, making sure they're properly seated and secured in the caliper. You may need to compress the caliper piston using a C-clamp or lug wrench to make room for the new pads.
7. Reinstall the caliper, making sure it's properly aligned and secured with the caliper screws. Tighten the screws in a star pattern to ensure even pressure.
8. Reinstall the wheel and lower the vehicle to the ground, then tighten the wheel nuts in a star pattern to ensure even pressure.
9. Test the brakes by pumping the pedal a few times and then driving slowly to a safe location. Make sure the vehicle stops smoothly and evenly, and that there are no unusual noises or vibrations.

Some common mistakes to avoid when replacing your own brake pads include:
- Not compressing the caliper piston enough, which can cause the new pads to not seat properly
- Not tightening the caliper screws evenly, which can cause the caliper to become misaligned
- Not inspecting the caliper and rotor for wear or damage, which can cause the new pads to wear out prematurely
- Not using the correct type of brake pads for your vehicle, which can cause poor braking performance or other safety issues

Tips for best results include:
- Making sure the vehicle is on a level surface and the parking brake is applied before starting work
- Using a torque wrench to tighten the caliper screws and wheel nuts to the correct specification
- Using a brake cleaner to clean out the caliper and rotor, and to remove any debris or residue
- Taking your time and working carefully, as brake repair can be messy and frustrating if you're not careful
- Considering upgrading to high-performance brake pads or rotors if you drive aggressively or tow heavy loads

By following these steps and taking the necessary precautions, you can replace your own brake pads and save around $200 in the process. Just remember to stay safe, work carefully, and don't be afraid to ask for help if you need it.
