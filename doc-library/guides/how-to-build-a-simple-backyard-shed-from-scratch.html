How to Build a Simple Backyard Shed From Scratch

Turn an empty corner of your yard into a functional storage paradise - with a sturdy, weather-resistant shed you built yourself in just a few weekends.

Building a backyard shed sounds intimidating, but it's one of the most rewarding DIY projects you can tackle. A simple shed gives you storage for tools, seasonal gear, and lawn equipment - freeing up space in your garage and basement. Best of all, you don't need advanced carpentry skills or expensive tools. This guide walks you through a basic 8x10 ft wooden shed design that's spacious enough to be useful, small enough to stay manageable, and designed to last for decades.

Before you dig your first post hole, you need a solid foundation. The most forgiving foundation for a beginner is a gravel pad with concrete footings. This approach costs less than a full concrete slab, drains water naturally, and actually keeps your shed structure healthier by allowing air circulation underneath.

Start by choosing your location. Pick a spot that's relatively level, gets good sun exposure (to prevent moisture and mold), and is at least 10 feet from any tree limbs or structures. Check local zoning laws - some areas require permits or setback distances from property lines. Call your utility company's "call before you dig" number (usually 811 in the US) to mark buried gas, electric, and water lines.
