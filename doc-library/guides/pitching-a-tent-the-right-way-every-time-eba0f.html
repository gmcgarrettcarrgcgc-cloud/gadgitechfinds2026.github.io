Camping Skills

Pitching a Tent the Right Way Every Time

Stop fighting with tangled poles and flapping fabric — learn the simple system experienced campers use to pitch a tent fast, keep it standing through wind and rain, and stay dry all night.

A tent that is pitched wrong will not just look sloppy — it can leak, collapse in wind, or leave you sleeping in a puddle at 2 AM. The good news? Pitching a tent right is not about strength or experience. It is about following the same simple sequence every time, in the right order, so nothing gets missed.

Here is the first half of that sequence.

1. Choose your site before you touch the tent. Look for flat ground, free of rocks, roots, and sticks. Avoid the lowest point in the area — that is where rainwater collects. Check overhead for dead branches that could fall. If you are near water, camp at least 200 feet back, both for safety from flash flooding and to follow Leave No Trace guidelines.

2. Clear and prep the ground. Remove any rocks, pinecones, or sticks under where the tent will sit — even small ones will jab you all night and can puncture the floor. If it is available, lay a groundsheet (footprint) down first; it protects the tent floor and adds a layer against ground moisture.

3. Orient the tent. Check the wind direction and try to point the smallest, most aerodynamic end of the tent into it, with the door facing away from the wind if possible. This one decision makes a huge difference in how much flapping and noise you deal with overnight.
