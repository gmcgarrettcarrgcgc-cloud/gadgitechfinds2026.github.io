Hunting Basics

How to Scout Hunting Land Like a Pro

Great hunters aren't lucky — they scout smart. Learn the simple, step-by-step system serious hunters use to find land loaded with game before you ever step foot in the woods.

Most beginners hunt where they've always hunted, or wherever a buddy says "there's deer out there." Pros do it differently — they scout on purpose, using a system, weeks or months before opening day. The good news: you don't need fancy gear or years of experience to start doing this too. You just need to know what to look for and where to look first.

Step 1: Start With Maps, Not Boots
Before you ever walk into the woods, scout from your couch. Use a free topographic map (USGS topo maps are free online) or a satellite map app like Google Earth to look at the land from above. You're hunting for three things: food, water, and cover, and how they connect. Look for edges — where a food source (like a field or clear-cut) meets thick cover (like a forest edge or brushy draw). Animals travel along these edges constantly, especially early morning and evening. Also look for "funnels" — narrow strips of woods between two open fields, a saddle between two hills, or a strip of trees along a creek. These pinch animal movement into a predictable path, which makes them prime spots to focus on later.
