How to Stay Warm and Dry Camping in the Rain

Rain does not have to ruin your camping trip — with the right setup, you can stay warm, dry, and comfortable all night long, even in a downpour.

Nothing ends a camping trip faster than a cold, soggy night in a leaking tent. But rain camping is not something to fear — it is something you prepare for. The secret is not expensive gear. It is understanding three things: what is actually letting water in, how your body loses heat when it is wet, and how to set up your camp so water flows away from you instead of into you.

Most "my tent leaked" stories are not really about a bad tent — they are about touching the tent walls from the inside (which pulls water through by capillary action), pitching on ground that collects runoff, or skipping a simple step like seam sealing. Once you know the real causes, fixing them takes minutes, not money.

Let us start with the single most important decision you will make before you even leave the trailhead: where you pitch your tent.
