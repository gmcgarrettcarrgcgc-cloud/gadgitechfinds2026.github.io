Camping Skills

Backpacking Checklist for First-Timers

Nervous about your first backpacking trip? This no-nonsense checklist tells you exactly what to pack, what to skip, and how to avoid the rookie mistakes that ruin a trip before it starts.

So you are planning your first backpacking trip. Exciting! But also a little terrifying, right? What if you forget something important? What if your bag weighs a ton and your shoulders hate you by mile two? What if you are cold all night because you did not bring the right sleeping bag?

Good news: every experienced backpacker was a nervous beginner once, and the fixes are simple once you know them. This guide walks you through exactly what to pack, organized the way your actual backpack should be organized, plus the most common first-timer mistakes and how to dodge them.

1. Start With the "Big Three"
Before anything else, get these three items sorted, because they matter most for comfort and safety:

- Backpack: 40-50 liters is plenty for a 1-3 night trip. Try it on loaded with weight before you buy — the hip belt should carry most of the load, not your shoulders.
- Shelter: A tent, or a lightweight tarp setup if you are comfortable with that. For your first trip, a simple 2-person backpacking tent is the easiest choice.
- Sleep system: A sleeping bag rated for temperatures colder than you expect, plus a sleeping pad (this insulates you from the cold ground — skipping it is the number one rookie mistake for cold nights).
