Campfire Cooking for Beginners: Simple Meals You Can Actually Make Outdoors

Turn any campfire into a kitchen — no fancy gear, no guesswork, just simple steps that get you a hot, delicious meal every time you camp.

There is nothing quite like eating a meal you cooked over your own campfire. But if you have never done it before, it can feel intimidating — how hot is the fire? What if you burn everything? What do you even need to bring?

Good news: campfire cooking is one of the easiest outdoor skills to learn, and you do not need much to get started. This guide walks you through everything step by step, starting with the fire itself.

Step 1: Build the right kind of fire. You do not cook over big roaring flames — those just burn your food black on the outside and leave it raw inside. Instead, you want a bed of hot, glowing coals. Build your fire at least 30-45 minutes before you plan to cook, using a mix of kindling and firewood, and let it burn down. When the flames die back and you see a solid layer of orange-red coals with a light gray ash coating, you are ready to cook. Push the coals into a flat, even layer roughly the size of your pan or grill grate.

Step 2: Gather simple gear. You only need a few basics: heavy-duty aluminum foil, a pair of long-handled tongs, a cast iron skillet or grill grate if you have one, oven mitts or a thick towel, and a small shovel or stick to move coals around. None of this is expensive, and foil especially can substitute for a lot of "official" cookware.
