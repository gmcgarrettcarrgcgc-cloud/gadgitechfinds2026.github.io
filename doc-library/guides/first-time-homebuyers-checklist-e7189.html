Renting & Real Estate Basics

First-Time Homebuyer's Checklist

Buying your first home should not feel like defusing a bomb blindfolded — this step-by-step checklist walks you through the entire process so you know exactly what to do (and what to watch out for) from your first search to closing day.

Buying a home for the first time is exciting — and also a little terrifying. There is a lot of money, paperwork, and unfamiliar vocabulary involved, and one missed step can cost you thousands of dollars or delay your move-in date by weeks. The good news: the process follows a predictable order, and once you know the steps, it stops feeling like a mystery.

Here is what the journey actually looks like, broken into stages.

1. Check your finances first. Before you even look at listings, pull your credit report (you can get one free copy per year from each bureau at annualcreditreport.com) and calculate your debt-to-income ratio. Most lenders want your total monthly debts — including the new mortgage — to stay under about 43% of your gross monthly income. Fix any credit report errors now; they take weeks to resolve and can raise your interest rate if left alone.

2. Save for more than just the down payment. Beginners often save only for the down payment and forget closing costs (typically 2-5% of the loan amount), moving expenses, an emergency fund, and a home inspection fee ($300-$500 on average). Budget for all of it before you start house hunting, not after you have found "the one."

3. Get pre-approved, not just pre-qualified. Pre-qualification is a quick estimate based on what you tell a lender. Pre-approval is a lender actually verifying your income, assets, and credit — it results in a letter sellers take seriously. Get pre-approved from at least two or three lenders so you can compare interest rates and fees.
