How to Rebuild a Small Engine Carburetor in Your Garage

Your mower, chainsaw, or generator won't start after sitting all winter? It's almost always the carb. Here's how to fix it yourself in under an hour - no mechanic, no $80 bill.

If a small engine cranks but won't start (or starts then dies), 90% of the time it's gummed-up fuel varnish clogging the carburetor jets - not the engine itself. Old ethanol gas turns into a sticky lacquer when it sits for months, and it clogs the tiny passages inside the carb.

What you'll need: a 10mm socket, a flathead and Phillips screwdriver, a can of carb cleaner spray, a small wire or guitar string for poking out jets, and a drain pan.

Step 1 - Shut off the fuel valve and drain the bowl. Most small engine carbs have a small bowl on the bottom held by a single bolt. Loosen it and let the old gas drain into your pan - smell it, if it's sour/varnishy, that confirms the diagnosis.
