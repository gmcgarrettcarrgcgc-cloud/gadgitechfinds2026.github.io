Homesteading

Starting a Backyard Homestead on Under 1/4 Acre

Grow your own food and build real self-sufficiency in a tiny space - here's exactly how to start, what to grow, and why even a 5,000 sq ft yard is plenty.

Most people think you need acres to homestead. You don't. A quarter-acre (about 10,000 square feet) is actually an ideal size to start - big enough to be productive, small enough to manage without becoming a full-time job. Whether you have an actual quarter-acre or even less, this guide shows you exactly where to begin.

Homesteading isn't about being perfect or totally self-sufficient overnight. It's about growing some of your own food, reducing what you buy, and understanding where things come from. Even a 5,000 sq ft yard (half a quarter-acre) can feed a family of four some of their vegetables year-round if you plan it right.

The three big wins on a tiny homestead are: a vegetable garden (where most of your calories come from), a small herb section (saves money and tastes amazing), and maybe a few chickens or rabbits if local laws allow. Each of these works on small land.

This guide walks through exactly how to set up each one, what you'll actually harvest, and how much time it really takes per week.
