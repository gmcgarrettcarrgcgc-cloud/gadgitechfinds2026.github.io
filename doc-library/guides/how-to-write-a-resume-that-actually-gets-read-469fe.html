How to Write a Resume That Actually Gets Read

Your resume is competing with hundreds of others. Learn the exact structure, formatting, and word choices that get past resume screeners and land you real interviews.

## How to Write a Resume That Actually Gets Read

### 1. Understand How Your Resume Gets Screened

Before you write a single word, you need to know what happens to your resume after you hit send.

First, it goes through an Applicant Tracking System (ATS). This is software that scans for keywords, checks formatting, and decides if your resume is worth showing to a human. If the ATS rejects it, a human never sees it. This is why formatting matters so much — it's not just about looking nice, it's about being readable by machines.

If your resume passes the ATS, a hiring manager or recruiter looks at it for about 6 seconds on a first pass. They're looking for one thing: Did you do this job before, or something very similar? If the answer jumps out in the first few seconds, they read more. If not, they move on.

**What this means for you:** Your resume needs to be formatted simply (so the ATS can read it), and your most important qualifications need to be immediately visible (so the human can see them in 6 seconds).

### 2. Choose the Right Resume Format

There are three main resume formats. Use this one:

**Reverse Chronological Format** — List your jobs starting with your most recent job first, going backward in time.

Why? Because it's the most common format, ATS systems know how to read it, and hiring managers expect it. Don't get creative here. Save creativity for the content.

**Layout Rules:**
— Use a simple, clean font like Arial, Calibri, or Times New Roman in size 10 or 11.
— Keep margins at 0.5 to 1 inch on all sides.
— Use bold and italics sparingly — only for your name, job titles, and company names.
— No graphics, no columns, no tables. These break ATS scanning.
— Save as a PDF or Word document. Check the job posting for what they want.
— Stick to one page if you have fewer than 10 years of experience. Two pages is okay if you have extensive experience.

### 3. Build the Right Resume Sections in the Right Order

Your resume should have these sections, in this order:

**Header (Your Name and Contact Info)**
Your name (font size 12-14, bold), phone number, email address, city/state (not your full address — safety first), and your LinkedIn URL if you have one. Optional: a personal website or portfolio link if it's professional. Nothing else. No photo, no "Objective" statement.

**Professional Summary (Optional — Skip It)**
Many people put a summary here. Don't. Most are generic filler ("Hardworking professional seeking a challenging role..."). Instead, use that space for your experience. If you do include a summary, make it 2-3 lines and tie it directly to the job description. Example: "Marketing manager with 5 years driving paid social campaigns that generated $2M+ in revenue. Specialized in healthcare sector."

**Experience (This Is Your Main Section)**
List each job starting with the most recent. For each job, include:
— Company name (bold)
— Job title (bold)
— Dates (Month Year — Month Year)
— City, State
— 3-5 bullet points describing what you did and the results

**The Bullet Point Formula:**
Use this structure for each bullet: [Action Verb] + [What you did] + [Result/Impact]

Example: "Redesigned the onboarding email sequence, reducing customer churn by 18% in Q2 2024."
