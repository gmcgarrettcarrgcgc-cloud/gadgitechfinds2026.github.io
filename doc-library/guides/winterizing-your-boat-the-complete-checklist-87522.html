Winterizing Your Boat: The Complete Checklist

Skip a single step and a hard freeze can crack your engine block or ruin your fuel system — this checklist walks you through every stage of boat winterization so nothing gets missed.

Winter is hard on boats. Water left in an engine block, water lines, or a livewell can freeze, expand, and crack expensive parts — sometimes costing thousands of dollars to repair. The good news: winterizing a boat is very doable yourself, even if you've never done it before, as long as you follow the steps in order and don't skip anything.

This guide breaks the whole process into five stages: fuel system, engine, water systems, battery and electronics, and exterior storage. Each stage has simple, numbered steps written for someone doing this for the very first time.

Start with the fuel system, because it needs time to circulate before you shut the engine down for winter. Add a fuel stabilizer to your tank (follow the bottle's ratio for your tank size), then run the engine for about 10 minutes so the treated fuel moves all the way through the fuel lines and carburetor or injectors, not just sitting in the tank.
