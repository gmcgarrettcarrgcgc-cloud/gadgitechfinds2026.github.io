How to Make Chicken Stock from Scratch

If you're tired of relying on store-bought chicken stock that's high in sodium and preservatives, it's time to learn how to make your own from scratch. Not only will homemade chicken stock elevate the flavor of your cooking, but it's also a great way to reduce waste and save money. With this guide, you'll be making delicious, healthy chicken stock like a pro in no time.

To make chicken stock from scratch, you'll need a few basic materials and tools. Here's what you'll need to get started:
- 2-3 lbs of chicken bones (you can use raw or cooked bones, but raw bones will produce a clearer stock)
- 2 carrots, chopped
- 2 celery stalks, chopped
- 2 cloves of garlic, minced
- 1 large onion, chopped
- 6-8 quarts of cold water
- 2 tbsp of black peppercorns
- 2 tbsp of dried thyme
- 1 bay leaf
- A large stockpot (at least 8 quarts in size)
- A fine-mesh strainer or cheesecloth
- A large bowl or container for cooling and storing the stock

Now, let's move on to the step-by-step instructions:
1. Preparing the ingredients: Start by chopping the carrots, celery, and onion. You can chop them roughly, as they'll be simmering in the stock for a long time and will break down completely. Mince the garlic and set it aside.
2. Roasting the bones: If you're using raw bones, you can roast them in the oven to enhance the flavor of the stock. Preheat your oven to 400F (200C). Place the bones on a baking sheet and roast them for about 30 minutes, or until they're lightly browned. This step is optional, but it will add depth and richness to your stock.
3. Searing the vegetables: In a large stockpot, heat a couple of tablespoons of oil over medium heat. Add the chopped onion, carrots, and celery, and saute them until they're lightly browned. This will bring out the natural sweetness in the vegetables and add flavor to the stock.
4. Adding the bones and aromatics: Add the roasted bones (if using), garlic, peppercorns, thyme, and bay leaf to the stockpot. Pour in the cold water, making sure that all the ingredients are submerged.
5. Bringing to a boil: Bring the stock to a boil, then reduce the heat to a simmer. You want to see a few gentle bubbles rising to the surface, but you don't want the stock to be boiling vigorously.
6. Simmering the stock: Let the stock simmer for at least 6-8 hours, or overnight. The longer you simmer it, the richer and more flavorful it will be. You can simmer it for up to 24 hours, but 6-8 hours is a good starting point.
7. Straining the stock: After the simmering time is up, remove the stockpot from the heat and let it cool slightly. Strain the stock through a fine-mesh strainer or cheesecloth into a large bowl or container. Discard the solids and let the stock cool to room temperature.
8. Cooling and storing: Once the stock has cooled, you can store it in the refrigerator for up to 5 days or freeze it for up to 6 months. You can also can the stock using a pressure canner, but that's a more advanced topic.

Common mistakes to avoid:
- Not using enough bones: The more bones you use, the richer and more flavorful your stock will be. Aim for at least 2-3 lbs of bones per 6-8 quarts of water.
- Not simmering the stock long enough: The longer you simmer the stock, the more collagen will be extracted from the bones, resulting in a clearer, more gelatinous stock.
- Not skimming the stock: As the stock simmers, a layer of fat and impurities will rise to the surface. Skim this off regularly to keep the stock clear and prevent it from becoming cloudy.

Tips for best results:
- Use a variety of bones: You can use a combination of raw and cooked bones, as well as different types of bones, such as neck bones, back bones, or wing bones.
- Add acidity: A splash of vinegar or lemon juice can help to extract minerals from the bones and add brightness to the stock.
- Experiment with aromatics: You can add other aromatics, such as leeks, parsley, or bay leaves, to the stock to give it more depth and complexity.
