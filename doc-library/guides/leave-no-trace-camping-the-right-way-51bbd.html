Leave No Trace: Camping the Right Way

Camp like a pro who leaves the wilderness exactly as beautiful as you found it — this guide breaks down the 7 simple principles that keep nature wild for the next camper (and keep you out of trouble).

Ever pulled up to a campsite and found trash, fire scars, or toilet paper scattered in the bushes? It ruins the whole experience — and it is completely avoidable. "Leave No Trace" is not some strict rulebook for hardcore outdoorsmen. It is seven common-sense habits that any beginner can learn in one trip. Get them right, and you will camp with a lighter footprint, fewer conflicts with rangers or other campers, and honestly, a cleaner, nicer spot to relax in.

The first principle is simple: Plan Ahead and Prepare. Before you even leave home, check the weather, know the regulations for where you are headed (some parks require permits or ban campfires during dry seasons), and pack a trash bag, a trowel, and reusable containers instead of single-use ones. A little planning prevents almost every Leave No Trace mistake before it happens.
