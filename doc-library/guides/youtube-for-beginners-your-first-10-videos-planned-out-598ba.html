YouTube for Beginners: Your First 10 Videos, Planned Out

Stop staring at a blank camera roll wondering what to post. This guide gives you 10 real video ideas, in the right order, plus the exact steps to film, edit, and publish your very first YouTube video this week.

Most people who want to start a YouTube channel quit before they upload a single video - not because they lack ideas, but because they never turn "I should start a channel" into "here's my first 10 videos, planned out." That's the gap this guide closes.

1. Pick Your Channel's One Topic (Not Five)
Before you touch a camera, decide what your channel is about - in one sentence. "Cooking easy dinners for busy people" is a topic. "Cooking, gaming, and my daily life" is five different channels fighting each other. New viewers decide whether to subscribe based on whether they know what they'll get next time. Pick ONE topic area for your first 10 videos. You can always branch out later once you have an audience who trusts you.

2. Understand the "First 10 Videos" Rule
Your first 10 videos are not about going viral. They're about proving to yourself you can finish and publish - and giving new visitors enough content that when they land on your channel, it looks active and worth following. Aim for consistency over perfection. A finished, imperfect video beats a perfect video that never gets uploaded.

3. The 10-Video Content Map
Here's a proven structure that works for almost any topic:
- Video 1: "Welcome + What This Channel Is About" (60-90 seconds, tells viewers exactly what to expect)
- Videos 2-4: Your "beginner basics" videos - the most searched, most basic questions in your topic
- Videos 5-6: A "how I do X" or behind-the-scenes video - builds personal connection
- Videos 7-8: A "top 5" or "common mistakes" list video - these format extremely well for search and suggested videos
- Video 9: Answer a real question a friend or family member asked you about your topic
- Video 10: A "30-day check-in" reflecting on what you've learned so far - this one builds trust because it's honest, not polished...
