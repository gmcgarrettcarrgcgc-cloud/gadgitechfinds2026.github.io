How to Choose the Right Wood for Any Project

Stop guessing and start building with confidence. Learn exactly which wood works best for your next project—from furniture to cutting boards to outdoor structures.

Picking the wrong wood can ruin a project before you even start. You might choose something too soft and watch it dent, or pick expensive hardwood for a task that doesn't need it. The right wood depends on three things: what the wood will do, what it looks like, and how much you want to spend.

Most projects fall into a few categories. If you're building something that sits indoors and just needs to look good, like a shelf or picture frame, you have tons of options. If you're making something people eat off of, like a cutting board or serving tray, you need food-safe wood that won't splinter or absorb stains. If you're building outside, your wood has to survive rain, sun, and temperature changes without rotting or warping.

The easiest way to think about wood is softwood versus hardwood. Despite the names, this isn't about how hard the wood actually is. Softwood comes from evergreen trees like pine, spruce, and fir. Hardwood comes from trees like oak, maple, and walnut. Softwood is cheaper, lighter, and easier to cut. Hardwood is denser, more durable, and usually more beautiful—but costs more and takes more work.
