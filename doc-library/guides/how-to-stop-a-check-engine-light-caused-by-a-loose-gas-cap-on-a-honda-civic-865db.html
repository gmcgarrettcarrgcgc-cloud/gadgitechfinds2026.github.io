How to Stop a Check Engine Light Caused by a Loose Gas Cap on a Honda Civic

That glowing check engine light doesn't always mean expensive trouble - half the time it's just a loose gas cap. Here's the exact fix, step by step, before you spend a dime at the shop.

If your Honda Civic's check engine light just came on and the car seems to be running totally fine - same power, same sound, no weird smells - there's a good chance the cause is something small and cheap: your gas cap. Modern cars, including every Honda Civic since the late 1990s, have a sealed fuel system called the EVAP system (short for "evaporative emissions"). Its job is to trap gasoline fumes inside the tank instead of letting them leak into the air. Your gas cap is the seal that keeps that whole system airtight. If the cap is loose, cracked, or not seated right, the car's computer notices the pressure change and lights up the check engine light to warn you. The most common trouble codes for this are P0455 (a large leak) and P0442 (a small leak) - both are standard, universal codes, not Honda-specific, so this fix works the same way whether you're driving a 2015 or a 2024 Civic.
