How to Negotiate a Higher Starting Salary

You're about to accept a job offer. But what if you asked for more money first? Learn the exact words, timing, and strategies that get offers increased by 5-20% without losing the job.

## How to Negotiate a Higher Starting Salary

### 1. Prepare Before You Even Interview

Negotiation starts long before an offer lands in your inbox.

**Research the salary range for your role**
Use Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, PayScale, and LinkedIn Salary to find what others in your role earn at that company and in your city. Look at at least 20 data points so you see the range, not just one number. Write down the low, middle, and high end of the range. Your counteroffer should land in the upper-middle, not the top (that looks unrealistic).

**Know your own minimum acceptable salary**
This is different from what they offer. It's the lowest amount you'd accept to leave your current job or take the risk on this new role. Know this number cold. It keeps you from accepting panic-level lowballs out of fear.

**Document your value before the interview**
Before you even talk to them, make a list of:
— Specific projects you've led and the measurable outcomes (revenue gained, time saved, team size managed)
— Skills that are rare or in-demand for this role
— Experience they probably can't easily replace
— Any awards, certifications, or publications relevant to the role

You won't recite this in the negotiation, but you'll feel more confident because you know exactly why you're worth the money.

### 2. The Offer Arrives: Use the 24-Hour Rule

You receive an offer (usually via email or call). Your instinct is to respond immediately. Don't.

**Tell them you need 24 hours to review the full offer**
Say this: "Thank you so much. I'm excited about this opportunity. I'd like to review the full offer in writing and get back to you tomorrow. Does that work?"

Almost every employer will say yes. This buys you time to:
— Review the entire package (salary, benefits, start date, stock options, sign-on bonus)
— Calm your emotions and think clearly
— Prepare your counteroffer
— Consult with someone you trust (a mentor, partner, or trusted friend)

### 3. Calculate Your Counteroffer

Use the research you did earlier.

**If the offer is below market rate**
Ask for the middle of your researched range. Example: If jobs like yours pay $60K-$75K, and they offered $58K, ask for $67K.

**If the offer is at market rate**
Ask for 5-10% more. This signals you've done your homework and aren't desperate. Example: If they offered $65K and market is $60K-$70K, ask for $69K-$71K.

**If the offer is above market rate**
Be careful. You can ask for a small bump (3-5%), but anything higher looks greedy. In this case, focus on other perks (extra PTO, work-from-home days, professional development budget, sign-on bonus).

**Don't ask for a specific dollar sign yet**
Start with a range. This gives you room to negotiate down without feeling like you lost. Example: "I'm thinking $68K-$72K based on my research and experience." They'll usually counter around $70K, and you've "negotiated" a win without actually taking a big cut.

### 4. The Conversation: What to Say

This usually happens on a phone or video call.

**Set the tone first**
Say: "I'm really excited about this opportunity. I want to make sure we both feel good about moving forward, so I wanted to discuss the compensation package."
