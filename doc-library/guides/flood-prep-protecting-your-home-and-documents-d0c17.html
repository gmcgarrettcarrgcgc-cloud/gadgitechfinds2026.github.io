Flood Prep: Protecting Your Home and Documents

Floodwater can ruin a home in hours — but a few hours of prep now can save your walls, your basement, and every important paper you own. Here is exactly what to do, in order, before the water ever rises.

Floods are the most common natural disaster in the United States, and they can happen almost anywhere — not just near rivers or coastlines. Heavy rain, melting snow, or a broken storm drain can flood a home that has never flooded before. The good news: most flood damage is preventable or reducible if you prepare ahead of time instead of scrambling during a storm warning.

This guide walks you through three things, in the order you should actually do them: (1) protecting the structure of your home, (2) protecting your important documents and valuables, and (3) what to do in the final hours before water arrives. You do not need special skills or expensive equipment for most of this — just a plan and about a weekend of setup time.

Step 1: Know Your Risk First
Before you buy a single sandbag, find out your actual flood risk. Go to FEMA Flood Map Service Center online and enter your address — it is free and takes two minutes. This tells you if you are in a high-risk flood zone, which matters for both preparation and insurance.
