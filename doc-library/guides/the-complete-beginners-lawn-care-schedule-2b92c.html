The Complete Beginner's Lawn Care Schedule

Tired of guessing when to mow, feed, or water your lawn? This simple month-by-month schedule takes all the guesswork out so you get a thick, green lawn all year long.

A great lawn isn't magic — it's timing. Most beginners fail because they do the right things at the wrong time of year, like fertilizing too early or mowing too short in summer heat. This guide breaks the whole year into simple seasonal steps anyone can follow, no experience needed.

1. Know Your Grass Type First
Before you do anything, figure out if you have a "cool-season" grass (like Kentucky bluegrass, tall fescue, or ryegrass — common in the northern half of the US) or a "warm-season" grass (like Bermuda, zoysia, or St. Augustine — common in the South). This matters because they grow on opposite schedules. Cool-season grass grows most in spring and fall. Warm-season grass grows most in late spring through summer. If you're not sure which you have, ask a local garden center or check your state's cooperative extension website — they can usually identify it from a photo.

2. Early Spring (as soon as the ground is no longer frozen and grass starts turning green)
- Rake up dead leaves, sticks, and matted grass (called "thatch") so sunlight and air can reach the soil.
- Do a light first mow once grass reaches about 3-4 inches — never cut more than one-third of the blade height at once, cutting too short stresses the lawn.
- If you had weed problems last year, this is the time for a "pre-emergent" weed control product, which stops weed seeds from sprouting. Follow the bag's instructions exactly — timing is everything with these products.
