How to Fillet Any Fish Like a Charter Captain

Master the pro technique that turns any catch into boneless fillets in under a minute. Learn the exact cuts and angles that separate amateurs from seasoned anglers.

Filleting a fish is one of the most practical skills you can learn as an angler or home cook. A proper fillet removes all the meat from the bones cleanly, wastes nothing, and looks professional. The good news? Once you understand the basic angle and three core cuts, you can fillet almost any fish - from a small bluegill to a large salmon.

What You'll Need:
- A sharp fillet knife (6-inch blade, flexible but not floppy)
- A clean cutting board with a groove to catch liquid
- Paper towels or a cloth to grip the fish
- A trash bowl for bones and scraps
- Running water or a bowl to rinse your hands between cuts

The knife is everything. A dull knife will slip, crush meat, and wreck your fillets. A sharp, slightly flexible blade glides through flesh and follows bone curves without tearing. If your knife is dull, sharpen it first - it actually makes the job easier and safer.

Getting Started:
Before you begin, place your fish on the cutting board with the head pointing away from you. Pat the surface dry with a paper towel; a slippery fish is a frustrating fish. Position your non-knife hand ready to stabilize the fish, but keep fingers tucked away from the blade.

The First Cut - Behind the Head:
This is where most people hesitate, but it's straightforward. Hold your knife at a 45-degree angle behind the fish's gills and pectoral fin. Make a diagonal cut from the top of the back down toward the belly, stopping when you feel the blade hit the spine. You're not cutting all the way through - you're just marking where the fillet separates from the body. This single cut determines everything that follows.
