Packing a Carry-On for a Week-Long Trip

Most people pack too much. A week-long trip doesn't need seven different outfits when you pick clothes that work together. The secret is thinking in "mix-and-match outfits" instead of individual pieces, and understanding TSA liquid rules so you're not fumbling at security.

Here's the complete system:

STEP 1: CHOOSE YOUR COLOR SCHEME

Pick 2-3 neutral base colors and commit to them. Good combinations:
- Black, white, and grey
- Navy, cream, and tan
- Black, olive, and grey
- Camel, white, and brown

Every single item you pack should work with every other item. This sounds restrictive, but it actually gives you way more outfit combinations than random pieces. A black top works with navy pants, grey pants, and black pants. A striped sweater works with your solid-colored bottoms. When your palette is tight, everything coordinates.

STEP 2: PACK BOTTOMS (3-4 PIECES)

- 1 pair of dark jeans (black or navy). These are your workhorse. Wear them two or even three times during the week.
- 1 pair of neutral pants or chinos. This gives you a non-denim option for slightly dressier days or just variety.
- 1-2 pairs of shorts or a skirt, depending on where you're traveling and the season.

Stop here. Three to four bottoms is genuinely enough. You're not going to run out of "new" looking outfits because you're changing your tops and accessories, and most people won't notice you've worn the same jeans before.

STEP 3: PACK TOPS (5-7 PIECES)

- 2 plain t-shirts in neutral colors (white, black, grey, or navy). These are your invisible foundation. Nobody remembers a plain t-shirt; they remember the jacket over it or the necklace with it.
- 2 casual tops with some personality. These could be printed t-shirts, simple blouses, striped shirts, or lightweight sweaters.
- 1-2 nicer tops if you're going somewhere that requires dressier outfits (work event, nice dinner, social occasion). Even a simple button-up counts.
- 1 lightweight tank top. This layers under everything and takes zero space.

Why lightweight matters: Heavy sweaters take up space and are hard to pack. Merino wool or cotton blends weigh less and dry faster if you hand-wash something. Avoid anything with beading, ruffles, or excessive texture — it wrinkles easily and takes up space.

STEP 4: ONE STRUCTURED LAYER

Pack one cardigan, blazer, or structured jacket. This serves two purposes: it keeps you warm on the plane and in over-air-conditioned spaces, and it instantly dresses up any outfit. Roll it tightly instead of folding to save space.

STEP 5: UNDERWEAR AND SOCKS

Pack one pair of underwear per day plus one extra. Seven days = eight pairs. Socks: same rule. If you're traveling to a hot climate, pack one extra pair of both — you may want to change midday.

STEP 6: SHOES (THE HARDEST PART TO PACK LIGHT)

Most people pack too many shoes. You need three at most:
- 1 versatile everyday shoe (white sneakers, leather loafers, or neutral sandals)
- 1 dressier option if needed (one pair of heels or leather shoes)
- 1 comfortable walking shoe if the everyday shoe won't hold up to heavy walking

Wear your bulkiest shoes on travel days to save space in your bag.
