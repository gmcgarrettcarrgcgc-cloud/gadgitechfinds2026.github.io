Changing Your Own Brake Pads: Save $200 in One Afternoon

You don't need a mechanic to replace your brake pads - and you'll save hundreds doing it yourself. Here's exactly how, step by step, even if you've never opened a toolbox before.

Brake pads are one of the easiest car maintenance jobs to do yourself, and it's one of the biggest money-savers. A mechanic charges $200-400 for this 30-minute job. You can do it for $30-60 in parts and keep the rest.

**What you need to know upfront:**
Brake pads wear down from friction every time you stop. When they're thin, your brakes don't work as well, and metal-on-metal noise gets louder. Replacing them is straightforward - no special skills required.

**What you'll need:**
- Jack and jack stand (or ramps)
- Lug wrench
- Socket wrench set (usually 17mm or 19mm for the caliper bolt)
- New brake pads (about $25-50 for a full set)
- C-clamp or old brake pad
- Flashlight (optional but helpful)

Everything else in this guide walks you through the exact process, safety checks, and common mistakes to avoid.
