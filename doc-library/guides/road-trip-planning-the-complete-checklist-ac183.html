Road Trip Planning: The Complete Checklist

Master the road trip from start to finish with this practical checklist. From vehicle prep to packing strategy to staying safe on the highway — never forget something important again.

## Road Trip Planning: The Complete Checklist

### Part 1: Prepare Your Vehicle (Do This 1 Week Before)

Your car is about to work harder than usual. Give it a fighting chance.

**Step 1: Check your tire pressure and condition**
Cold tires hold less air. Check pressure first thing in the morning (when tires are cold) and adjust to the PSI listed on a sticker inside your driver's door — not the maximum on the tire itself. Look for cracks, bulges, or uneven wear on each tire. If you see damage or tread is worn (less than 4/32 inch depth — use a penny test: stick a penny upside down into the tread; if you can see the top of Lincoln's head, your tread is too shallow). Worn tires are dangerous on long drives, especially in rain. Replace them before you go.

**Step 2: Top off all fluids**
Pop your hood and check: engine oil (should be between min/max marks on the dipstick), coolant (don't touch the radiator cap when the engine is hot — you can get burned), brake fluid, and windshield washer fluid. Top off anything that's low. Bring extra coolant and oil for the trip — you can buy small bottles at any gas station or auto parts store.

**Step 3: Test your brakes**
Find an empty parking lot and do a few gentle stops, then a few firm stops. Brakes should feel smooth and responsive, not spongy or uneven. If they feel wrong, don't wait — get them checked by a mechanic before you leave.

**Step 4: Check your battery**
Most car batteries last 3-5 years. If yours is older, have it tested at an auto parts store (free). A dead battery 300 miles away is a miserable experience.

**Step 5: Test your air conditioning and heating**
You don't want to discover your AC doesn't work when you're driving through Arizona in July. Turn it on now and feel the air. Same for heat — crank it up and make sure warm air flows.

**Step 6: Check your spare tire and jack**
Find the spare (usually under the trunk floor or on the back of the vehicle). Make sure it's not flat. Locate the jack and lug wrench. Practice using them once in your driveway so you're not fumbling in an emergency.

### Part 2: Pack Smart (The Day Before)

Overpacking turns a car into a sauna and drains gas mileage. Underpacking leaves you stranded without essentials. Here's the balance.

**Step 1: Make a packing list by category**
Documents (licenses, insurance cards, registration, any hotel confirmations), Medications (full bottles, not just loose pills), Comfort (pillow, blanket, change of clothes), Electronics (phone charger, car charger, power bank), Food (snacks, water, electrolyte drinks), Tools (jumper cables, basic toolkit, flashlight, first aid kit), Emergency (spare cash, AAA membership card if you have one, phone numbers written down).

**Step 2: Pack a proper emergency kit**
Jumper cables, flashlight with extra batteries, basic toolkit (hammer, pliers, screwdrivers, wrench), duct tape, spare fuses, jumper pack (portable battery for your car), warning triangles or flares, first aid kit, blanket, water bottles, granola bars. This kit could save your trip or your life.

**Step 3: Organize your snacks**
Pack more water than you think you need. Bring electrolyte drinks (Gatorade, coconut water) — they hydrate better than water alone during long drives. Pack nuts, fruit, granola bars, and sandwiches. Avoid sugary snacks that cause energy crashes. Bring gum or mints to stay alert.

**Step 4: Know the weight limit**
A heavily loaded car burns more gas and puts extra strain on your suspension and tires. Aim to fill no more than about 75% of your trunk.
