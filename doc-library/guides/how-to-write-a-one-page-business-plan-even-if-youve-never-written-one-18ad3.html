How to Write a One-Page Business Plan (Even If You've Never Written One)

You do not need 40 pages, a business degree, or weeks of your life — just one clear page can turn a rough idea into a real, fundable, followable plan in under an hour.

Most people think a "business plan" means a thick binder full of charts nobody reads. It does not. A one-page plan does the same job — it forces you to think through your idea, your customer, and your money — but it fits on a single sheet of paper you will actually use.

Here is the truth: banks, investors, and even you six months from now do not need 40 pages. They need clear answers to a handful of important questions. This guide walks you through exactly what those questions are and how to answer them, one box at a time.

Step 1: Write your business in one sentence.
Before anything else, describe what you do in a single sentence a stranger could understand. Example: "I sell handmade dog collars to pet owners who want something sturdier than what is in stores." If you cannot say it in one sentence, your idea is still too fuzzy — and that is fine, this exercise will fix that.

Step 2: Name the problem you are solving.
Every business exists because it solves a problem or fills a want. Write down, in plain words, what frustrates or is missing for your customer right now. "Store-bought dog collars break within a year" is a real problem. Be specific — vague problems lead to vague businesses.
